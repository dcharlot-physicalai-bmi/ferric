Reactor number four at the Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant, then in the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic, suffered a catastrophic power excursion during a late-night safety test on 26 April 1986. The graphite fire that followed burned for nine days and carried fallout across much of Europe.

The partial meltdown at Three Mile Island in Pennsylvania occurred in March 1979. Although no deaths were attributed to it, the accident reshaped nuclear regulation in the United States and halted new plant orders for decades.

A tsunami generated by the Tohoku earthquake overwhelmed the seawall at Fukushima Daiichi in March 2011, disabling the emergency generators and leading to three core meltdowns. The plant sits on Japan's Pacific coast.

Tungsten is a refractory metal that melts at 3,422 degrees Celsius, the highest of any element. Its symbol, W, comes from wolfram, the name still used for it in German and several Slavic languages.

Molybdenum, symbol Mo, is often alloyed with steel to raise its strength at high temperature. It sits directly above tungsten in the periodic table and shares many of its properties at lower cost.

Titanium, symbol Ti, is prized for a strength-to-weight ratio that beats steel, and for resisting corrosion in seawater. It is named after the Titans of Greek mythology.

Jupiter completes one rotation in just under ten hours, faster than any other planet in the Solar System. That speed flattens the planet noticeably at the poles and drives the banded cloud structure visible through a small telescope.

Venus rotates so slowly, and in the opposite direction to most planets, that a single Venusian day lasts longer than its year. Its dense carbon-dioxide atmosphere keeps the surface hot enough to melt lead.

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun, orbiting once every 88 Earth days. It has almost no atmosphere, so its surface swings hundreds of degrees between day and night.

An adult human skeleton contains 206 bones. A newborn has closer to 300, many of which are cartilaginous and fuse during growth, which is why the adult count is lower than the infant one.

The human body contains 32 teeth in a full adult set, including the four wisdom teeth that often have to be removed. Children have 20 primary teeth before these are replaced.

The femur is the longest and strongest bone in the human body, running from hip to knee. The stapes in the middle ear is the smallest, at roughly three millimetres.

Canberra was purpose-built as the seat of Australian government after Sydney and Melbourne could not agree which should hold it. The Parliament House there opened in 1988, replacing a provisional building from 1927.

Sydney is Australia's largest city by population and the capital of New South Wales, but it has never been the national capital despite hosting the 2000 Summer Olympics.

Wellington, at the southern tip of New Zealand's North Island, is the world's southernmost national capital. Auckland is the larger city.

Crowds began dismantling the Berlin Wall on the night of 9 November 1989, after an East German spokesman announced, prematurely, that border crossings were open. Formal German reunification followed the next year.

The Berlin Airlift kept West Berlin supplied by air for eleven months from June 1948, after Soviet forces blockaded road and rail access to the western sectors of the city.

The Soviet Union was formally dissolved in December 1991, when the leaders of Russia, Ukraine and Belarus declared it had ceased to exist and the flag over the Kremlin was lowered.

Water boils at 212 degrees Fahrenheit at standard atmospheric pressure, which is 100 on the Celsius scale. The Fahrenheit scale places the freezing point at 32 rather than zero, so the interval between the two points is 180 degrees.

Absolute zero is minus 273.15 degrees Celsius, the temperature at which a system reaches its minimum internal energy. No experiment has ever reached it, though laboratories have come within billionths of a degree.

The Kelvin scale shares its degree size with Celsius but starts at absolute zero, so water freezes at 273.15 K and boils at 373.15 K under standard pressure.

The element with atomic number 79 is gold, symbol Au from the Latin aurum. Its resistance to corrosion and its conductivity make it useful in electronics far beyond its ornamental value.

Silver, atomic number 47, is the best electrical conductor of any element, slightly better than copper. It tarnishes in air, which limits its use in exposed contacts.

Platinum, atomic number 78, sits beside gold in the periodic table and is rarer in the Earth's crust. It is a catalyst in vehicle exhaust systems and in many industrial reactions.

Titan is the largest of Saturn's moons and the second largest in the Solar System. It is the only moon known to have a dense atmosphere, and lakes of liquid methane sit on its surface.

Ganymede, orbiting Jupiter, is the largest moon in the Solar System and is bigger than the planet Mercury. It is the only moon known to generate its own magnetic field.

Enceladus, a small icy moon of Saturn, vents plumes of water vapour from fractures near its south pole, evidence of a liquid ocean beneath the ice.

Apple announced its first iPhone in January 2007 and put it on sale that June. It shipped without an application store, which arrived the following year.

The iPad was introduced in 2010, defining the modern tablet category after several earlier attempts by other manufacturers had failed to find a market.

The first commercially successful personal computers appeared in 1977, when the Apple II, the Commodore PET and the TRS-80 all reached buyers within months of each other.

Tokyo hosted the Summer Olympics of 1964, the first Games held in Asia. They were the first to be broadcast internationally by satellite and introduced the shinkansen to visitors.

The 1936 Summer Olympics were held in Berlin, where Jesse Owens won four gold medals. The Games were used extensively for propaganda by the host government.

Athens hosted the first modern Olympic Games in 1896, with fourteen nations competing. The city hosted them again in 2004.

Challenger Deep, at the southern end of the Mariana Trench in the western Pacific, is the deepest known point in any ocean, close to 10,900 metres below the surface.

The Mariana Trench forms where the Pacific Plate subducts beneath the Mariana Plate. It stretches roughly 2,550 kilometres and averages 69 kilometres wide.

Mount Everest rises 8,849 metres above sea level, the highest point on land. Measured from base to summit rather than from sea level, Mauna Kea in Hawaii is taller.

Albert Einstein published the general theory of relativity in 1915, extending his earlier special theory to include gravity as the curvature of spacetime. Its prediction of light bending near the Sun was confirmed during a 1919 eclipse.

Isaac Newton set out his laws of motion and universal gravitation in the Principia of 1687. His description of gravity remained unchallenged for more than two centuries.

Quantum mechanics was assembled in the 1920s by Heisenberg, Schrodinger, Born and Dirac among others, and describes matter at atomic scale where classical mechanics fails.

The smallest prime number is 2, and it is the only even one, since every other even number is divisible by it. One is not prime, because a prime is defined as having exactly two distinct divisors.

There are infinitely many prime numbers, a result Euclid proved by showing that any finite list of them must be incomplete. The largest known primes today are Mersenne primes with tens of millions of digits.

A perfect number equals the sum of its proper divisors: 6 and 28 are the first two. Whether any odd perfect number exists is still unresolved.

Human skin synthesises vitamin D when ultraviolet B radiation strikes it, converting a cholesterol derivative into the precursor of the active hormone. Deficiency causes rickets in children.

Vitamin C, or ascorbic acid, cannot be synthesised by humans and must come from the diet. Its absence causes scurvy, which killed more sailors in the age of sail than combat did.

Vitamin B12 is found almost exclusively in animal products, which is why long-term vegan diets usually require supplementation to avoid nerve damage.

The currency of Japan is the yen, introduced in 1871 to replace the complex Tokugawa-era system. Its symbol is a Y with two horizontal strokes.

The Chinese renminbi, whose primary unit is the yuan, has been managed against a basket of currencies since 2005 rather than pegged solely to the dollar.

The South Korean won replaced the hwan in 1962. Like the yen, it is normally quoted in whole units rather than in decimal subdivisions.

The Second World War ended in 1945, with Germany surrendering in May and Japan in September after the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet declaration of war.

The First World War ended with an armistice on the eleventh hour of 11 November 1918. The Treaty of Versailles formally concluded it the following year.

The Korean War began in 1950 and was halted by an armistice in 1953. No peace treaty was ever signed, so the two Koreas remain technically at war.

Diamond is the hardest naturally occurring substance known, scoring 10 on the Mohs scale. It is pure carbon in a tetrahedral lattice, and only laboratory materials such as certain boron nitrides approach it.

Graphite is also pure carbon, but its atoms lie in sheets that slide over one another, which makes it one of the softest common minerals and a useful dry lubricant.

Corundum, the mineral of ruby and sapphire, ranks 9 on the Mohs scale, well below diamond despite being the next hardest common mineral.

Nitrogen makes up about 78 percent of the Earth's atmosphere by volume, with oxygen at roughly 21 percent and argon most of the remainder. Nitrogen is largely inert at ambient temperature, which is why it dominates without being consumed.

Carbon dioxide is a trace component of the atmosphere at around 0.04 percent by volume, yet it dominates discussion of the climate because it absorbs infrared radiation the surface emits.

The ozone layer sits in the stratosphere between roughly 15 and 35 kilometres up, and absorbs most of the Sun's ultraviolet B before it reaches the ground.

The Amazon is the longest river in South America and carries more water than any other river on Earth, discharging roughly a fifth of all river water reaching the oceans.

The Nile drains eleven countries in northeast Africa and has long competed with the Amazon for the title of longest river in the world, depending on where each source is judged to begin.

The Danube flows through more countries than any other river, ten in all, from the Black Forest in Germany to the Black Sea.

A standard violin has 4 strings, tuned in fifths to G, D, A and E. The viola and cello use the same interval pattern a fifth and an octave-plus-fifth lower.

The modern classical guitar carries six strings, tuned mostly in fourths. Steel-string and electric instruments follow the same tuning, though seven- and eight-string variants exist.

A concert harp has 47 strings and seven pedals, each pedal altering every string of one pitch class across the instrument's range.

The Indian Ocean lies between Africa to the west and Australia to the east, bounded by Asia to the north. It is the warmest of the major oceans and the third largest by area.

The Atlantic Ocean separates the Americas from Europe and Africa, and is widening by a few centimetres a year as new crust forms along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.

The Pacific is the largest and deepest ocean, covering more of the Earth's surface than all land combined, and is ringed by the volcanic and seismic belt known as the Ring of Fire.
